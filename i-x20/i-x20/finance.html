Sharia-compliant solutions

Property finance with principle

Buying property shouldn't mean compromising your values. Here's how Sharia-compliant financing works, explained in plain language with real-world examples.

Traditional interest-based mortgages (forbidden in Islamic finance) are replaced by structures that share profit and risk fairly. No hidden interest, no deception—just straightforward agreements built on transparency and shared value.

01

Murabaha

Cost-Plus Purchase

The finance provider buys the property on your behalf, then sells it to you at an agreed markup (their profit). You pay a fixed amount each month. Simple, transparent, no interest charges.

Real-World Example

Scenario: You want to buy a flat worth £250,000. You have £50,000 saved.

How Murabaha works:

1
The Sharia bank purchases the flat for £250,000
2
The bank immediately sells it to you for £290,000 (£250,000 cost + £40,000 profit markup, agreed upfront)
3
You pay your £50,000 down payment
4
You pay the remaining £240,000 in fixed monthly installments over 25 years (about £800/month)

Key benefit: You know exactly what you'll pay each month—no surprises. The markup is disclosed and agreed before you sign anything. No interest compounding, just straightforward cost-plus pricing.

Best for: People who want simplicity and certainty. You know your total cost upfront and never have to worry about interest rate changes.

02

Diminishing Musharakah

Shared Ownership Partnership

You and the finance provider jointly own the property. Over time, you gradually buy out the provider's share while they earn rental income from their portion. Eventually, you own 100%.

Real-World Example

Scenario: Same £250,000 flat. You have £50,000. The bank has £200,000.

How it works:

1
You and the bank co-own the property. You own 20% (£50k), they own 80% (£200k)
2
You both agree on rental value: £1,200/month
3
Each month, the bank receives its share of rent (80% = £960) as profit on their investment
4
You pay the bank £1,000/month to buy their share gradually (this reduces both the rent they receive and increases your ownership)
5
Over 25 years, your ownership grows to 100% and theirs shrinks to 0%

Key benefit: It's fair to both parties. The provider earns profit (rent) on their money while you gradually gain full ownership. If property value increases, you both benefit proportionally.

Best for: People who want to feel like gradual partners in ownership growth. Works well if you believe the property will increase in value—you both share that gain.

03

Ijarah

Lease-to-Own Financing

The finance provider owns the property and you lease it from them for an agreed term (usually 15-25 years). At the end of the lease, ownership transfers to you (or you continue as a renter).

Real-World Example

Scenario: Same £250,000 flat.

How it works:

1
The bank buys the flat and owns it entirely (you don't own it yet)
2
You lease it for 20 years at £1,300/month (covering the bank's costs + fair profit)
3
The lease agreement includes a transfer clause: at year 20, ownership automatically passes to you
4
After 20 years, you own the property outright and owe nothing more

Key benefit: Clean separation. You lease while the bank owns. No co-ownership complications. The transition to full ownership is automatic and pre-agreed.

Best for: People who prefer a straightforward lender/renter relationship and want ownership to transfer cleanly at the end of a fixed term.

Quick Comparison

Murabaha

  • Provider buys, then sells to you
  • Markup known upfront
  • Fixed monthly payment
  • Full ownership from day 1
  • Simplest structure

Musharakah

  • Shared co-ownership
  • You gradually buy provider's share
  • Provider earns rental profit
  • Share property appreciation together
  • Most partnership-like

Ijarah

  • Provider owns, you lease
  • Ownership transfers at lease end
  • Fixed lease payments
  • Clean handover process
  • Most rental-like

Common Questions

01. Why isn't this just regular interest?

In Islamic finance, you're not being charged interest (riba). Instead, you're paying for goods, services, or shared profit. In Murabaha, you're paying a known markup on cost. In Musharakah, the bank earns rental income proportional to ownership. In Ijarah, you're paying fair rent. The key difference: no compounding interest, and pricing is always transparent and fair from the start.

02. Are these options regulated?

Yes. Sharia-compliant finance in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and follows UK property law exactly like conventional mortgages. The difference is internal structure, not regulatory status. Your property rights are identical.

03. Can I switch between structures?

Generally, once you commit to a structure, you stay with it for the term. Some providers offer flexibility at refinancing points, but this is rare. Choose carefully upfront.

04. Which is cheapest?

Total cost depends on market rates, your credit profile, and provider economics. Murabaha often has the lowest total cost because it's simplest. Musharakah may be more expensive (rental income to provider increases cost). Ijarah depends on the lease terms agreed. Get quotes from multiple providers for your situation.

05. Can I repay early?

Most structures allow early repayment without penalty, but confirm with your provider. Some structures (especially Musharakah) may have complications if property value has changed significantly.
